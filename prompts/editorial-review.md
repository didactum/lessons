# Editorial Review Checklist (`read` → `revise`)

Before submitting a core lesson PR to `didactum/lessons`, run one teacher-review pass over your draft against these six criteria and revise once:

1. **Slide 1 Problem Setup (Zero Spoilers):** Does Chapter 1 immediately recite the concrete problem aloud and pin BOTH the visual diagram and the exact question (`?`) so a learner immediately knows what is being solved — without spoiling the final answer?
2. **Cumulative Chalkboard Buildup:** Does the main derivation accumulate 4–6 progressive chalk steps on ONE working pane (rather than wiping after 1–2 sparse lines), and does it pin the final boxed answer back into the top pinned band?
3. **Teacher Voice, Not Textbook:** Are chapter titles vivid and lesson-specific (never `Setting the Scene`, `The Trap`, `Solving It`, `Why It Matters`, `Conclusion`)? Are spoken lines breathable with intentional `(pause)` beats and plain-language restatements of pivotal insights?
4. **Visible Wrong-Move Failure:** Is the most tempting misconception tried on the board (`in coral`), shown visibly failing, and struck out?
5. **Woven History, Humor & Real-World Stakes:** Are historical origins (verified facts only), dry warm teacher wit, and authentic real-world uses woven naturally into the setup, trap, and derivation — never bolted on as a separate summary appendix after the problem is solved?
6. **Math, Speech & Boundary Precision:** Are all `TRUTH_CHECKS` verified, `transfer_key` kept strictly `host-only` (never spoken or hinted), `SAY:` lines free of raw symbols and place-value slips (e.g. never "thirty tens"), and zero phrases copied verbatim from other lessons?
